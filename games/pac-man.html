What Is Pac-Man?

Pac-Man is a maze arcade game created by Toru Iwatani and originally released by Namco in 1980. The player moves Pac-Man through a maze filled with small pellets, bonus fruit and larger power pellets. Four ghosts patrol the corridors and try to catch him. Clear every regular pellet to complete the maze and advance to the next stage.

The rules are easy to understand, but surviving requires planning. Corridors can become dangerous when ghosts approach from different directions. A good player watches the maze, chooses escape routes and saves power pellets for moments when the ghosts are close. The combination of simple movement and constant decision-making is why Pac-Man remains a classic arcade experience.

Meet the Four Pac-Man Ghosts

The ghosts are more than colorful obstacles. Each has a distinct personality and movement behavior in the original game. Learning their names makes it easier to follow guides and understand classic Pac-Man strategy discussions.

  • Blinky – the red ghost: commonly known as the direct pursuer, Blinky is the most recognizable threat and tends to chase Pac-Man aggressively.
  • Pinky – the pink ghost: tries to position itself ahead of Pac-Man, making turns and intersections important.
  • Inky – the cyan ghost: has more complex targeting behavior that depends on Pac-Man and another ghost's position.
  • Clyde – the orange ghost: alternates between chasing and moving away depending on its distance from Pac-Man.

What Are Power Pellets?

Power pellets are larger items placed around the maze. When Pac-Man eats one, the ghosts temporarily become vulnerable and change appearance. During this short period, Pac-Man can eat ghosts for bonus points instead of running away. The effect does not last forever, so use the opportunity wisely and avoid getting trapped when the ghosts return to their normal state.

Power pellets are especially useful near intersections where several escape routes are available. If you activate one while a ghost is close, you may be able to turn a dangerous situation into a scoring opportunity. Try not to waste the effect by moving into an empty corner with no ghosts nearby.

Pac-Man Strategy Tips for Beginners

  • Look ahead: plan your next turn before reaching an intersection.
  • Know your exits: avoid entering narrow dead ends when ghosts are close.
  • Save power pellets: use them when they can help you escape or collect bonus points.
  • Keep moving: standing still gives approaching ghosts more time to surround you.
  • Learn the maze: repeated play helps you remember routes and safer loops.
  • Watch multiple ghosts: focus on the nearest threat but keep track of the others.
  • Use tunnels: side passages in classic maze layouts can help you cross to the opposite side.
  • Collect fruit when safe: bonus items are useful, but do not risk a life for them unnecessarily.

Why the Maze Matters

The maze is not just a background. Its corridors, corners and intersections determine how safely Pac-Man can move. Long corridors make it easier to see a ghost approaching, while tight turns can create opportunities to escape. Learning the layout allows players to choose routes that collect pellets efficiently without becoming surrounded.

Some classic versions include side tunnels that connect opposite sides of the maze. These passages can be valuable escape routes because they change the distance between Pac-Man and the ghosts. Understanding when to use a tunnel is an important part of improving your survival time.

Why Pac-Man Is Still Popular

Pac-Man has remained part of gaming culture for decades because its core idea is timeless. The maze is immediately understandable, the characters are memorable and every run presents a new set of decisions. Players can enjoy it casually or challenge themselves to clear stages efficiently and improve their high score.
