What Is Tetris?

Tetris is a tile-matching puzzle game designed by Alexey Pajitnov and first released in 1984. The playing field is a vertical rectangle called the Matrix. Seven different shapes, known as Tetriminos, fall from the top. Each piece is made of four square blocks. Your task is to move and rotate each falling shape so that it fits with the blocks already placed on the board.

When a horizontal row is completely filled with no empty spaces, that line clears and the blocks above it move down. Clearing lines creates space and earns points. The game becomes more demanding as the level rises and pieces fall faster. A round ends when the stack reaches the top and there is no room for a new piece to enter.

The Seven Tetriminos

Every standard Tetris game uses seven familiar shapes. Learning how each shape fits into gaps is the foundation of good play. The long I piece can clear four lines at once, while the square O piece is useful for filling compact spaces. The remaining shapes offer different ways to fill corners, ledges and uneven surfaces.

  • I piece: the long straight shape, useful for clearing four lines in one move.
  • O piece: the square shape, ideal for filling a two-by-two space.
  • T piece: a versatile shape that can fit into central gaps and support advanced spins.
  • L piece: a three-block vertical side with one block extending at the bottom.
  • J piece: the mirrored counterpart of the L piece.
  • S piece: a zigzag shape that fits certain stepped surfaces.
  • Z piece: the opposite zigzag shape, useful for filling matching gaps.

  4. Drop carefully: use a soft drop for control or a hard drop when the landing is clear.

How Tetris Scoring Works

Scoring rewards clearing one or more lines at the same time. In the official browser version, a single line clear, double, triple and four-line Tetris have different base values, and the current level multiplies the score. Soft drops and hard drops can also award points based on the distance the piece travels. Special moves such as T-spins may provide additional scoring opportunities in versions that support them.

A four-line clear is often called a Tetris. To make one, keep a vertical well open on one side of the board and build the rest of the stack without covering that column. When an I piece appears, rotate it vertically and drop it into the open space. This can clear four rows at once if the stack is prepared correctly.

Beginner Tetris Strategy Tips

  • Keep the stack low: a low, even surface gives you more choices for upcoming pieces.
  • Avoid unnecessary holes: gaps buried under blocks are difficult to fill and can force risky placements.
  • Build with a plan: look at the next-piece preview when available and prepare a suitable landing space.
  • Leave room for the I piece: if you are aiming for a four-line clear, maintain a clean vertical well.
  • Do not rotate at the last second: move pieces early enough to avoid misdrops.
  • Use soft drop for precision: when a placement is tight, slow down and confirm the position.
  • Clear lines before the stack becomes dangerous: saving every piece for a Tetris can backfire.
  • Practice all seven shapes: learn how each one fits different surfaces and gaps.

What Is a Tetris?

In Tetris terminology, a Tetris is a special four-line clear. It happens when one I-shaped Tetrimino fills a vertical column of four empty cells while the other nine columns of four rows are already occupied. This is a high-value move in many classic scoring systems. Building a clean well and waiting for the long piece is a common strategy, but it is important not to let the rest of the stack grow too high while waiting.

Why Tetris Gets Faster

Many Tetris modes increase the falling speed as you clear lines or advance through levels. Faster pieces reduce the time available to decide where to place each shape. This is why good players rely on consistent stacking habits instead of making every decision from scratch. As you improve, try to recognize useful gaps quickly, keep the surface relatively flat and avoid creating deep holes that require specific pieces to repair.

Why Tetris Remains a Classic Puzzle Game

Tetris is easy to learn because its objective is immediately clear, but it remains challenging because every placement changes the shape of the board.
